Chemolithotrophy: How Microbes "Eat" Rocks to Live

00 The Intuitive Primer: How Biological Electricity Works

🔋 The Flashlight Battery Analogy (No Biochemistry Background Needed!)

Every living cell on Earth is essentially a chemical battery:

  • The Negative Terminal (Electron Donor): This is the food. It holds onto electrons loosely and wants to throw them away (e.g. H₂, Fe²⁺, or glucose).
  • The Wire (Electron Transport Chain / ETC): The cell passes electrons down a wire of proteins in its membrane. As electrons travel down, they release energy to pump protons outside the cell.
  • The Positive Terminal (Terminal Electron Acceptor): This is the vacuum or sponge that greedily catches the used electrons at the bottom (usually O₂ from the air we breathe!).
  • The Lightbulb (ATP Synthase): Protons rush back inside through a tiny spinning turbine to charge up ATP (the cell's universal rechargeable battery).

How Humans / Animals Do It

Chemoorganotrophs ("Organic Eaters"): We strip electrons from organic carbon foods (bread, glucose, lipids) and throw them onto oxygen (O₂) to make ATP.

How Chemolithotrophs Do It

Chemolithotrophs ("Rock Eaters"): They strip electrons from inorganic minerals or gases (H₂, H₂S, Fe²⁺, NH₃) and throw them onto O₂ or nitrate to make ATP!

01 The 4 Separate Questions of Microbial Metabolism

Beginners often confuse energy with carbon. In microbiology, these are answered by 4 completely independent questions:

1. What gives electrons?

Litho vs. Organo

Lithotroph: Inorganic donor (H₂, Fe²⁺, H₂S, NH₃).
Organotroph: Organic donor (glucose, acetate).

2. Where does energy come from?

Chemo vs. Photo

Chemotroph: Chemical oxidation reactions.
Phototroph: Sunlight photons.

3. Where does body carbon come from?

Auto vs. Hetero

Autotroph: Builds body from scratch using CO₂ gas.
Heterotroph: Absorbs ready-made organic food.

4. Where do used electrons go?

Aerobic vs. Anaerobic

Aerobic: Thrown onto O₂ gas.
Anaerobic: Thrown onto NO₃⁻, SO₄²⁻, or Fe³⁺.

💡 In Plain English: What is a "Chemolithoautotroph"?
Breaking down the word part by part: Chemo (gets energy from chemicals) + Litho (electron donor is a rock/gas, not sugar) + Autotroph (makes its own body from CO₂ gas in the air).

02 The Electron Tower & Thermodynamic Feasibility

🌊 Waterfalls and Electrons: Understanding Reduction Potential (E₀′)
Think of the Electron Tower like a mountain with a waterfall:
  • Molecules at the top (negative Volts, e.g. H₂ at −0.41 V) are high up on the cliff. They drop electrons easily.
  • Molecules at the bottom (positive Volts, e.g. O₂ at +0.82 V) are at the bottom of the canyon. They catch falling electrons greedily.
  • The farther electrons fall (the larger the voltage gap ΔE₀′), the more energy (ΔG°′) is released to make ATP!
ΔG°′ = − n · F · ΔE₀′ = − n · (96.485 kJ/V·mol) · (E₀′acceptor − E₀′donor)
Simple Translation: (Number of electrons) × (Physics constant) × (Height of the waterfall drop) = Total usable energy!

03 The Central Puzzle: Reverse Electron Flow (RET)

💰 The Difference Between Energy (ATP) and Construction Materials (NADH)

To build a house, you need two things: Money (ATP) to pay the workers, and Bricks & Cement (NADH) to lay the walls.

  • CO₂ gas is oxidized carbon. To turn it into cell body parts (proteins, DNA, cell walls), you must force hydrogen atoms and electrons onto it.
  • The cell's universal electron delivery truck is NADH (E₀′ = −0.32 V).
  • If your food is Hydrogen (H₂ at −0.41 V): It's higher than NADH! You can just hand electrons directly to NAD⁺ downhill for free!
  • If your food is Iron (Fe²⁺ at +0.77 V) or Nitrite (NO₂⁻ at +0.43 V): It is far below NADH! Electrons naturally want to fall down, not jump up.
🔄 The Solution: Pumping Water Back Up the Hill
To make NADH, iron and nitrite bacteria must run a biological pump in reverse! They let most electrons fall to oxygen to pump protons out (Proton Motive Force / Δp), and then spend that battery power to force electrons backwards uphill into NAD⁺ to make NADH. This is called Reverse Electron Transport (RET).

06 Real-World Lab: Acid Mine Drainage & Gold/Copper Biomining

⛏️ How Bacteria Dissolve Mountains to Mine Copper and Gold

Pyrite (FeS₂, "Fool's Gold") is trapped inside rocks deep underground. When mining exposes it to air and moisture, iron-oxidizing bacteria (Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans) move in:

  • They oxidize dissolved ferrous iron (Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺) 100,000 times faster than air alone!
  • The produced ferric iron (Fe³⁺) is a ferocious acid oxidant that attacks surrounding copper and gold ores, dissolving them into solution so miners can collect them (Biomining).
  • However, this also generates oceans of concentrated battery acid (H₂SO₄, pH < 2) and orange iron rust crusts called "Yellowboy" that pollute rivers (Acid Mine Drainage).

09 Step-by-Step Mathematical Calculations (Simplified)

Clear, step-by-step math breakdowns showing how much energy these organisms actually harvest:

Problem 1: Why Iron Oxidizers Must Eat Tons of Rust to Make One Gram of Body Mass

Question: At pH 2.0, the reduction potential of iron is +0.77 V, and oxygen is +0.82 V.

  1. Calculate the voltage drop (ΔE₀′) and energy released (ΔG°′) per mole of iron oxidized.
  2. If making 1 mole of carbon body mass costs ~ 480 kJ of energy, how many kilograms of iron must the bacterium turn over?
Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Voltage drop: ΔE₀′ = +0.82 V − (+0.77 V) = +0.05 V (Tiny! Like a dying 0.05V battery!).
Energy: ΔG°′ = −(1) × (96.485) × (0.05 V) = −4.82 kJ/mol Fe²⁺.

2. Iron mass required:
Moles = 480 kJ / 4.82 kJ/mol ≈ 99.6 moles Fe²⁺.
Mass = 99.6 mol × 55.85 g/mol ≈ 5,562 grams (5.56 kg) of iron!

What this means: The bacterium must eat over 5.5 kilograms (12 pounds!) of liquid iron just to grow 12 grams of cellular carbon. That is why iron oxidizers produce gigantic mountains of orange rust!
Problem 2: Comparing Hydrogen Gas (Knallgas) vs. Rocket Fuel (Anammox)

Question: Compare the energy release of burning hydrogen gas vs. anaerobic ammonium oxidation.

Step-by-Step Solution:
• Knallgas Reaction: H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O
ΔE₀′ = 0.82 − (−0.41) = +1.23 V  →  ΔG°′ = −237.4 kJ/mol H₂ (Fiercely explosive energy!).

• Anammox Reaction: NH₄⁺ + NO₂⁻ → N₂ + 2H₂O
ΔE₀′ = 1.00 − (−0.28) = +1.28 V  →  ΔG°′ = −370.5 kJ/mol NH₄⁺ (Even more energetic than hydrogen!).
Problem 3: Why Hydrogen Bacteria Don't Pay the "Reverse Flow" Energy Tax

Question: Explain why Cupriavidus necator has an unfair advantage over iron and nitrite bacteria.

Step-by-Step Solution:
1. The cell needs electrons delivered to NAD⁺ at −0.32 V.
2. Hydrogen gas (H₂) sits way up high on the tower at −0.41 V.
3. Because H₂ is higher than NAD⁺, the bacterium's Soluble Hydrogenase (SH) enzyme simply transfers electrons directly into NAD⁺ for free without spending any ATP or proton battery power!

12 Quick Summary: The Big Picture Takeaways

What You Need to Remember

  • Chemolithotrophy = Eating inorganic chemicals (H₂, H₂S, Fe²⁺, NH₃, NO₂⁻, CO) for energy instead of organic sugars.
  • Energy vs. Carbon are separate: Chemolithotrophs can build their own bodies from CO₂ (autotrophs) or eat organic scraps (heterotrophs/mixotrophs).
  • Reverse Electron Flow (RET) = When your food is lower on the electron tower than NADH, you must spend battery power (Δp) to push electrons backwards uphill to make NADH for building cell walls and DNA.
  • Hydrazine (N₂H₄) = Real rocket fuel created inside a special dense organelle (the anammoxosome) by Anammox bacteria to produce N₂ gas.
  • Comammox = Bacteria like Nitrospira inopinata that perform the entire nitrification process from ammonia all the way to nitrate in a single cell.
