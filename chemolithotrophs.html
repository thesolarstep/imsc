Chemolithotrophs: Physiological Groups

Energy without organic food — how microbes strip electrons from rocks, gas, and rust to live.
What can a microbe eat if there is no organic food?
No sugars. No amino acids. No organic carbon at all. Some microbes still thrive — by oxidizing inorganic chemicals like H₂, H₂S, Fe²⁺, or NH₃ for energy.

01 What is chemolithotrophy?

Chemolithotrophy is the use of inorganic chemicals as electron donors to generate energy (ATP), via an electron transport chain — exactly as chemoorganotrophs do with organic compounds, and phototrophs do with light-driven electron flow.

⚠ Chemolithotrophy describes only the energy/electron source. Whether the organism is autotrophic depends on its carbon source — a separate axis entirely. Most chemolithotrophs are chemolithoautotrophs (CO₂-fixing), but some are chemolithoheterotrophs (organic carbon, inorganic energy). Don't collapse the two.

02 Four questions that separate the concepts

1. What gives electrons?Electron donor — defines "litho" vs "organo"
2. Where do electrons go?Terminal electron acceptor — O₂, NO₃⁻, etc.
3. Where does energy come from?The donor→acceptor redox reaction — ATP via ETC
4. Where does carbon come from?CO₂ (autotroph) or organic C (heterotroph)

06 Ecological significance

Nitrification
Ammonia and nitrite oxidizers drive the global nitrogen cycle, converting NH₃ to NO₃⁻ in soils and wastewater treatment.
Sulfur cycling
Sulfur oxidizers detoxify H₂S in sediments and are central to hydrothermal vent food webs.
Iron cycling & AMD
Iron oxidizers like Acidithiobacillus drive acid mine drainage and biomining operations.
Primary production in the dark
Chemolithoautotrophs support entire ecosystems (deep sea vents, caves) with no sunlight input.

09 One-minute recap

  • Chemolithotrophs oxidize inorganic electron donors (H₂, H₂S, Fe²⁺, NH₃, NO₂⁻) for energy.
  • Energy source (donor→acceptor) and carbon source (CO₂ vs organic) are independent axes.
  • Chemolithoautotroph = inorganic energy + CO₂ carbon. Chemolithoheterotroph exists too — don't assume all chemolithotrophs fix CO₂.
  • Know the five groups: H₂, S, Fe, NH₃ (ammonia), NO₂⁻ (nitrite) oxidizers — and their representative genera.
  • Ecologically, these groups drive N, S, and Fe cycles and support life without sunlight.
